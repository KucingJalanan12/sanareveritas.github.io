Is Vaping Safer Than Smoking?

Investigations August 2026

Edited by Felicia Abigail

Introduction

Over the past decade, electronic cigarettes, or vapes, have become increasingly popular as an alternative to conventional cigarettes. Vapes are often marketed as a “healthier” substitute for smoking and even as a tool to help people quit smoking. The main reason behind this perception is that vaping does not involve burning tobacco. Instead, it heats an e-liquid to produce an aerosol that is then inhaled by the user. The claim that vaping is “safer” has sparked a long-standing debate: is vaping actually safer than conventional cigarettes, or do the risks simply appear in different forms?


Definition and Brief History

Electronic cigarettes were first introduced to the public in 2004 by a Chinese company. Electronic cigarettes have the ability to produce vapor rather than smoke, which contributed to their popularity among people who wanted to quit traditional smoking. Over time, the presence of vaping has also influenced public health policies in various countries. One example is the United Kingdom, which recognizes vaping as a harm-reduction tool for adult smokers who want to quit. Aggressive marketing and the growing appeal of vaping, such as a wider variety of flavors and practical device designs, have encouraged its rapid adoption worldwide, including among young people who had never smoked before.


Vaping and Its Effectiveness in Smoking Cessation

A Cochrane systematic review, one of the most rigorous and continuously updated reviews, by Lindson et al. (2025) concluded that nicotine-containing electronic cigarettes are likely to help more smokers quit compared with conventional nicotine replacement therapies such as nicotine gum or patches. This finding is also supported by a systematic review by Vyas et al. (2024), which evaluated the effectiveness of vaping as a smoking cessation intervention among adults, as well as a network meta-analysis by Thomas et al. (2021) comparing the effectiveness and safety of various smoking cessation therapies, including vaping.
However, researchers emphasize that this effectiveness does not mean vaping is completely safe. The benefits of vaping for smoking cessation generally apply significantly to active smokers who switch completely from tobacco cigarettes to vaping, rather than to individuals who have never smoked. Dual use, meaning using vapes while continuing to smoke tobacco cigarettes, does not appear to provide significant health benefits compared with smoking alone.


Vaping and Cardiovascular Health

In terms of cardiovascular health, a systematic scoping review by Izquierdo-Condoy et al. (2024), which examined various studies on the direct effects of vaping, found that vaping is generally associated with a lower cardiovascular risk profile than conventional cigarettes, but it is by no means risk-free. Vaping can still acutely increase blood pressure, heart rate, and arterial stiffness, although these effects are generally smaller than those caused by conventional tobacco cigarettes.
This is supported by research conducted by Biondi-Zoccai et al. (2019), which compared the acute effects of heat-not-burn tobacco products, electronic cigarettes, and conventional tobacco cigarettes on the cardiovascular system. The study found that all three types of products could acutely increase blood pressure compared with baseline conditions. Similarly, Gonzalez and Cooke (2021) found that vaping can acutely disrupt peripheral sympathetic nervous system activity, which may contribute to hypertension, sweating abnormalities, and digestive problems. These findings indicate that although vaping may have a lower long-term cardiovascular risk than conventional cigarettes, it still produces significant acute physiological effects on the heart and blood vessels and is not completely neutral to cardiovascular health.


Vaping and Respiratory Health

In addition to the cardiovascular system, the respiratory system is also directly affected by vape aerosol. A systematic review by Mughis et al. (2024), which specifically examined the toxicity mechanisms of ENDS on the respiratory system, found that vape aerosol can cause harm through various pathways, including inflammation, tissue hypoxia, stress on the cardiovascular system, and metabolic changes. Vaping can also cause side effects such as throat irritation, coughing, nausea, and changes in hemodynamic function. This means that although vaping does not produce smoke from tobacco combustion like conventional cigarettes, its aerosol still contains compounds that can irritate and damage the respiratory tract.
Izquierdo-Condoy et al. (2024) also noted that although the risk of respiratory problems among vape users is generally lower than that of long-term tobacco smokers, several cases of serious acute lung injury, including those known as EVALI (E-cigarette or Vaping product use-Associated Lung Injury), have been reported, particularly in connection with illegal or modified vaping products containing harmful additives. This emphasizes that the relative safety of vaping depends not only on the type of device but also on the quality and authenticity of the product being used, which cannot always be guaranteed.


Conclusion

Based on a review of various studies, the claim that “vaping is safer than smoking” has scientific support only under certain conditions. Although vaping can reduce the use of conventional tobacco cigarettes, it can also become a new source of nicotine dependence, especially among young people. Vapes can contain carcinogenic compounds, and their use can have acute effects on the cardiovascular system, such as increased blood pressure and heart rate. Vaping can also cause inflammation and irritation of the respiratory tract and may lead to acute lung injury. Therefore, vaping is better understood as a product that may carry relatively lower risks for adult smokers who want to switch from conventional cigarettes, rather than as a completely safe product. Vaping is not entirely safe, particularly for individuals who have never smoked and for adolescents who are vulnerable to nicotine addiction through vaping.
